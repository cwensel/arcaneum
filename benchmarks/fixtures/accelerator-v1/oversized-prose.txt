An oversized input is valuable even when a small CI fixture cannot reproduce production scale. It forces every backend adapter to report how it handles an input outside its preferred shape budget: accept it, split it according to a documented policy, or reject it explicitly. Silent truncation would make numerical comparisons meaningless. This paragraph therefore carries enough varied language to differ materially from the short and medium fixtures. It describes cold model loading, warm batch inference, queue waiting, native compilation, upload time, and total end-to-end time as separate observations. It also names failures, restarts, fallbacks, timeouts, finite outputs, dimensions, vector norms, and cosine similarity because throughput alone is not evidence of a reliable backend. Qualification results must identify the operating system, processor architecture, memory capacity, dependency versions, model, precision, and device. A result from one concrete combination cannot establish a blanket claim for every accelerator.
